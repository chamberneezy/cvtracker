## Positioning Frames by Role Type

The default Core Positioning above (Analytical Digital Marketing & Data Governance Leader) fits most roles. For **pure technical e-commerce or product-owner roles** — especially at industrial/B2B companies where the audience is IT/platform-minded rather than marketing-minded — switch to the **Technical Platform Frame** instead:

- **Tone down** "Paid Media Spend" and campaign-budget keywords (ROAS, CAC, CPL, media planning, agency governance) — they read as marketing-spend overhead to this audience, not platform capability.
- **Lead aggressively** with the MSc in IT, JavaScript/TypeScript execution, API/data-layer architecture, and platform QA. These go first in the summary and skills groupings, not buried after marketing metrics.
- **Vocabulary substitution:** translate tracking/tagging language into platform-engineering language so it reads as IT platform management, not marketing ops:
  - "Tracking Signals & GTM Containers" → **"API Integrations, System Interface Quality, and Platform Release Testing"**
  - Apply the same translation logic elsewhere in the CV/cover letter when a term would otherwise signal "marketing tooling" instead of "platform engineering" — e.g. frame data-layer work as API/data architecture, frame A/B testing QA as release/platform QA.
- Decide which frame applies during Step 1 (Fit & Hook Analysis) and carry that choice through Step 2's Summary, Skills groupings, and Experience bullets, and into the cover letter's technical-edge paragraph.

## Trigger & Workflow Instructions

When the user pastes a Job Description (JD) or URL content:

### Step 1: Fit & Hook Analysis (Brief Summary)
1. **Fit Score (/10):** Direct assessment of role match.
2. **Positioning Frame:** Which frame applies — default Core Positioning, or the Technical Platform Frame (see "Positioning Frames by Role Type" above) for pure technical e-commerce/product-owner roles. State which one and why.
3. **Target Title Alignment:** Exact title string to use on the CV header (e.g., `PAID MEDIA PERFORMANCE MANAGER` or, under the Technical Platform Frame, `TECHNICAL PRODUCT OWNER — E-COMMERCE PLATFORM`).
4. **Strategic Hooks & Gaps:** Highlight 3 core strengths to leverage and frame any minor gaps (e.g., framing Adobe stack via general CDP/SQL/Schema concepts).

### Step 2: Adjusted CV Content Modules
Provide copy-pasteable, ATS-optimized markdown modules matching the candidate's exact single-column CV structure:
- **HEADER TITLE:** Aligned directly to job posting.
- **SUMMARY / ÜBER MICH:** 4-5 line power-packed intro linking MSc IT background, 10+ years experience, and specific core requirements of the job.
- **SKILLS / KOMPETENZEN:** Grouped into 4-5 targeted domain categories reflecting keywords from the JD (e.g., Media Governance, Paid Platforms, Analytics/SQL, Stakeholder Steering). Under the Technical Platform Frame, lead with API/Data Architecture and Platform QA groupings instead, and apply the vocabulary substitution (see "Positioning Frames by Role Type").
- **EXPERIENCE BULLETS:** High-impact, metric-driven bullet points for recent roles (**Swiss Casinos**, **20 Minuten**, **Adssential**, **LightLounge**), enriched with exact keywords from the posting. *Never truncate experience details—provide deep, specific bullets.* Under the Technical Platform Frame, reweight bullets toward API integrations, system interface quality, and platform release testing rather than paid media spend.

### Step 3: Tailored Cover Letter (Anschreiben / Motivation Letter)
Write like a real person, not a template. This is the part most likely to read as AI-generated if approached mechanically — avoid that specifically:
- **Vary structure and opening between letters.** Never reuse the same fixed phrases across applications (e.g. don't open every letter with a "the opportunity to do X is exactly what draws me" formula, and don't close every gap-disclosure with the identical "I want to be upfront" phrasing). Letters can end up similar by coincidence; never by formula.
- **Prefer flowing paragraphs over a bulleted "beyond the core requirements" list.** A real letter reads as connected thought building toward a point, not a feature list transplanted from the CV with bold lead-ins. Weave the evidence into sentences instead.
- **Open with something specific and genuine about the company or role** — pull a real detail from the JD or what the company actually does, not a generic template hook that could apply to any employer.
- **Keep it warm and direct.** Vary sentence length and rhythm; let one sentence be short. In German, favor shorter sentences with everyday connectors (und, weil, deshalb, dazu kommt) over stacked subordinate clauses and heavy nominalization — that formality is a giveaway on its own.
- **Calibrate German to the candidate's real level — B1/B2, not C1/native.** Use vocabulary and sentence structures an upper-intermediate speaker would naturally produce. The letter's fluency must not contradict what the CV states and what an interview will reveal — a native-sounding letter followed by B1/B2 spoken German is a worse impression than an honestly-calibrated one. When German proficiency is relevant to the role, it's fine to name the B1/B2 level directly and naturally (not as a disclaimer, just a fact) rather than let the prose imply more than it should.
- **Still name real gaps honestly** (per the project's established honesty convention — see CLAUDE.md's job-application workflow), but phrase the disclosure differently letter to letter instead of a fixed template sentence.
- 3–4 short paragraphs, professional sign-off. Length should feel like something a person wrote in one sitting, not a maximized keyword surface.

### Step 4: Supplementary Application-Form Answers (when a portal asks its own questions)

Many application portals (Workday, SmartRecruiters, Personio, jobs.ch, company-hosted forms) ask their own free-text questions beyond CV + cover letter — "Why do you want to work here?", "What's your salary expectation?", motivation/fit questions, weakness/challenge prompts, availability. When the user pastes these, answer them directly rather than pointing back at the cover letter. What HR actually responds well to, versus what reads as filler:

- **Answer the literal question in the first sentence.** Recruiters skim dozens of these; reward the ones that get to the point immediately instead of building up to it.
- **Concrete and quantified beats enthusiastic and generic**, same principle as the CV bullets — "I increased conversion from 2% to 8%" lands, "I'm passionate about digital marketing" doesn't. Pull the actual number from the Career History above rather than restating the job ad's language back at them.
- **"Why this company / this role?"** — anchor to something specific and true about *them* (their product, market position, mission, the JD's own stated challenge) plus a genuine link to the candidate's background. Never generic flattery ("I've always admired your innovative culture") — that's the single most common tell of a copy-pasted answer, and experienced HR readers spot it immediately.
- **Salary expectation / start date / workload questions** — use the captured ground-truth values above. If not yet captured, ask the user once (don't guess a number), then save the answer to the Candidate Profile section so future forms reuse it automatically.
- **Weakness / challenge / gap questions** — same honesty convention as the cover letter's gap-disclosure paragraph: name a real limitation and how it's being addressed, never invent a humble-brag fake weakness ("I work too hard").
- **Respect the field's length.** Portal fields are often character-limited or expect 2-4 sentences, not a paragraph — cut to the point rather than trimming a longer draft awkwardly.
- Never fabricate an experience, tool, or credential to answer a question more impressively — same rule as everywhere else in this workflow.

## ATS Formatting & Execution Principles

1. **Keyword Density:** Embed high-weight nouns from the JD (*e.g., Jahresmediaplanung, Plan-Ist-Abgleich, CAC, CPL, AEP, SQL, Consent Mode*) naturally throughout the summary, skills, and experience.
2. **Zero Filler:** Avoid generic fluff like "highly motivated worker." Lead every line with concrete technical actions, platform names, and business outcomes.
3. **Single-Column Compatibility:** Format text cleanly without multi-column markdown tables so it translates smoothly into ATS-friendly single-column PDFs.
